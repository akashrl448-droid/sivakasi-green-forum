Who We Are

Sixteen years of working the water, not just studying it.

Sivakasi Green Forum began as a handful of volunteers mapping a single silted-over reservoir — today we run restoration crews, awareness campaigns, and community stewardship programs across the region.

Our Story

Born from a single dry reservoir

Sivakasi Green Forum was founded in 2020 by a small group of residents, students, and local business owners who watched the industrial city's water reserves and green cover shrink year after year. What started as informal weekend clean-up drives at one depleted lake grew into a structured, community-led environmental organization.

We work within the limits set by nature — addressing green issues, advancing environmental principles, and restoring the ecosystems that Sivakasi and its surrounding villages depend on. Every project we take on unites businesses, government bodies, students, and social organizations around a shared goal of environmental protection.

We don't just plan from a desk. Through active participation, public campaigns, and physical volunteering, we bring restoration directly to the dry water reserves and depleted green covers in our home region — measuring our progress in acres restored and wells identified, not reports filed.

Our History

How we got here

2020

Founded in Sivakasi

A group of volunteers begins mapping and cleaning a single silted-over reservoir on weekends, with no formal structure — just shared concern for the city's disappearing water bodies.

2021

Registered as a formal organization

Sivakasi Green Forum is formally registered, bringing together a founding leadership team and its first structured desilting project at Sengulam Kanmai.

2022

First large-scale afforestation drive

Partnering with local schools and businesses, the Forum plants its first Miyawaki-method urban mini-forest, establishing a model repeated across later projects.

2024

Government & institutional partnerships

Local government bodies formally back the Forum's well-identification and water-table monitoring work, expanding its reach to villages beyond Sivakasi town.

2026

2,000+ trees, 42 acres restored

Today, the Forum runs ongoing restoration, monitoring, and community education programs across the Delta watershed region — with volunteers at the center of every project.

What Drives Us

Our Objectives

Restore Degraded Ecosystems

Bring back native tree cover, wetlands, and water bodies that have been depleted by industrial and urban growth.

Long-Term Monitoring

Track water tables, soil health, and species presence over years, not just at the moment a project is announced.

Community Stewardship

Train residents, students, and volunteers to maintain restored sites themselves, not depend on us indefinitely.

Public Awareness

Run campaigns, school programs, and open volunteer days that make environmental protection visible and approachable.

Institutional Partnerships

Work alongside local government, businesses, and civic bodies so restoration outlasts any single funding cycle.

Prevent Further Deterioration

Identify at-risk sites early — before a reservoir dries out or a green cover disappears entirely.

Where We Work

Areas of Work

01

Afforestation & Urban Forests

Miyawaki-method mini-forests and native species plantation drives across depleted green cover.

02

Water Body Restoration

Desilting, sewage separation, and shoreline revival at lakes, ponds, and reservoirs.

03

Groundwater & Well Mapping

Identifying, testing, and documenting public wells to guide sustainable water use.

04

Environmental Education

School programs and public workshops that build long-term environmental awareness.

05

Volunteer Field Programs

Structured weekend and monthly volunteering that puts hands directly on restoration work.

06

Policy & Government Liaison

Working with local bodies to align civic planning with long-term ecological priorities.

Community Impact

Measured in the community, not just acres

Restoration only lasts if the people living alongside it are invested in keeping it that way. Every project we run trains local volunteers, works with nearby schools, and hands off long-term maintenance to the community itself — so the impact doesn't end when our crew leaves the site.
